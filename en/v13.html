The key concept

Heart coherence

Your heart doesn't beat regularly. The interval between two beats varies constantly. This variation is called heart rate variability (HRV).

High HRV indicates a flexible nervous system, capable of adapting to stress. Low HRV suggests a rigid nervous system, stuck in alert mode.

Heart coherence is an optimal state where your heart rate variation becomes regular and synchronized with your breathing.

What are the scientific sources?

Heart coherence has been studied since the 1990s, notably by the Institute of HeartMath. Studies published in journals like Applied Psychophysiology and Biofeedback, International Journal of Psychophysiology, and Global Advances in Health and Medicine document its effects.

Why 6 breaths per minute?

It's the resonance frequency of the cardiovascular system for most adults (it can vary between 4.5 and 7). At this frequency, heart rate variability amplitude is maximal, which amplifies vagus nerve activation.

Does it work for everyone?

The physiological effect (heart-breath synchronization) is universal. The magnitude of benefits may vary depending on individuals and their regular practice.
